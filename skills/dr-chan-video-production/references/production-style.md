# Dr Chan production style

## Editorial identity

- Build trust through precise explanation, confident delivery, and visual clarity.
- Keep energy through pacing, visual variation, useful diagrams, and concise emphasis—not exaggerated medical claims or frantic effects.
- Let Dr Chan remain present during explanation. When B-roll replaces the main frame, use selected live talking-head bubbles so the audience continues to read his expression and authority.

## Typography and captions

- Preferred family: `Noto Sans HK`; preferred visible weight: ExtraBold/800 only after the renderer proves that face is actually loaded.
- Use large, clear Traditional Chinese captions with white primary text, a dark navy/black outline or shadow, and fluorescent-yellow emphasis on selected keywords.
- Synchronise karaoke emphasis to the word or short phrase Dr Chan is currently saying. Do not turn the whole caption line yellow unless the current brief explicitly requests it.
- Keep captions to one or two lines. Break at complete Cantonese phrases; never split a fixed medical term, number plus unit, negation, or verb-object phrase.
- Keep the approved caption baseline consistent across manual and automatic captions. Compare a manual card against the first unaffected automatic card instead of copying raw `top` values: different renderers may align text differently inside equal boxes.
- Prefer the existing project's approved lower-chest/lower-third caption band. Do not cover the face or an essential gesture.
- Do not silently shrink all captions because one long sentence overflows. Fix that sentence's wrapping, width, or copy first.

## Framing and screen variants

Use a deliberate mix of:

1. Tight head-and-shoulders A-roll.
2. Full-screen topic B-roll with captions.
3. Full-screen B-roll with a live circular talking-head bubble.
4. Full-screen B-roll with a rounded rectangular live talking-head inset.
5. Vertical mechanism/list/stat cards with Dr Chan retained as a live bubble when useful.
6. Brief full-frame Dr Chan shots for identity and direct trust moments.

Reduce prolonged full-body seated shots. A face bubble must use the moving talking-head video, not a still image. Use a true circular crop/mask inside the yellow ring; remove square or rounded-rectangle corners that protrude behind it. Enlarge the bubble when the frame otherwise feels empty, while protecting captions and the face.

- Each finished reel should normally show Dr Chan as a source-synchronised live talking head about 3–5 times. Alternate the large yellow circle and rounded-rectangle treatments instead of repeating one form throughout.
- Mute the talking-head overlay copy so it never doubles programme audio. Keep Dr Chan's face comfortably centred inside the crop.
- Treat clean stock as an automatic talking-head opportunity, not a stock-only default. When a stock shot has a simple background, no competing human face/head, and enough negative space, add Dr Chan in a safe upper or side zone.
- This clean-stock rule also applies to opening/title stock. Leave a clean stock shot full-screen only when a human-led or complex frame, or a medical/product detail, genuinely needs the whole canvas; record that reason during QA.
- Keep a deliberate safe margin from every edge. Never cover subtitles, MG information, products, logos, UI, or another person's face/head. If the stock is busy or human-led, move the talking-head occurrence to a cleaner moment rather than forcing it.

## Opening and title frames

- Design the first frame as a usable Facebook thumbnail: make the core title clear and immediately readable.
- Keep the series label and main title visually joined.
- On A-roll, centre the main title around the tie/chest region when it does not cover Dr Chan's face or the approved caption band.
- On clean opening stock, keep the joined title readable and place Dr Chan in unused upper or side negative space from the first frame where both can coexist.
- Do not place routine dialogue subtitles in the first `0.1 s`.

## B-roll

- Choose B-roll that directly explains the spoken point: blood glucose, insulin, pancreas, food/drinks, obesity, body measurement, medical mechanisms, or the exact lifestyle example being discussed.
- Match literal spoken examples whenever practical: tofu should show tofu; chicken breast or another named protein should show recognisable protein food; water should show a glass or visible water.
- Use enough variation to prevent a static talking-head reel, but do not replace every sentence. For a roughly 90–110 second reel, 8–15 purposeful B-roll or graphic changes is a useful starting range, not a quota.
- Review the surrounding shots before inserting stock. Do not place identical or near-identical clips beside each other; vary subject, angle, colour, and motion.
- Preserve continuity by keeping Dr Chan's voice uninterrupted. Use a live face bubble on selected B-roll, not every cutaway.
- Check licensing, subject suitability, on-screen text, and whether the source image implies a diagnosis, demographic claim, or health outcome that Dr Chan did not state.

## Motion Graphics

- Design for the 9:16 canvas first. Convert wide three-step or multi-box graphics into vertical stacks, timelines, ladders, or columns.
- Horizontal submodules are allowed inside a vertical composition, but the overall information flow must read vertically without tiny type.
- Default to transparent-overlay MG over stock or A-roll. Do not add a full-frame outer bounding box, baked MG background, or decorative side line unless it has a clear explanatory purpose and matches an approved reference.
- Place compact Super MG above the subtitle band, above the head, or in available negative space. It may overlap Dr Chan's body, but not his face or important gestures.
- Favour mechanism-led graphics: three-step cause/effect, rising blood-sugar scale, ranked food examples, warning statistics, and short quote cards.
- Use recognisable icons, object cut-outs, or images at the relevant marker when a point names a concrete food, drink, organ, or object and the visual improves comprehension. Avoid repeating the same pointing/list-card form.
- Place a live talking-head circle or rounded rectangle beside MG only when it does not cover the graphic, subtitle band, or platform safe area.
- Animate card entries with restrained flips, pops, or directional reveals tied to speech. Avoid decorative motion with no explanatory job.

## Visual pacing

- Do not allow roughly 15 seconds of uninterrupted pure A-roll.
- At or before that threshold, add relevant stock, stock plus a live talking head, or a context-specific vertical Motion Graphic at a natural spoken beat.
- Do not reset the pacing clock for subtitle highlighting, a routine punch-in, minor reframing, or colour correction alone.

## Colour and grade

- Core palette: deep navy, white, fluorescent yellow; use warm orange only as a restrained border/accent inherited from the approved visual look.
- Keep Dr Chan's skin natural and the navy suit rich without crushing shadow detail or oversaturating blue.
- Match shot-to-shot white balance and exposure before applying a look. Avoid a cold generic hospital grade unless the current project explicitly asks for it.

## Audio

- Keep Dr Chan's voice as the anchor. A previous approved working level used roughly `+5 dB`, but always confirm peak/headroom and perceived loudness on the current source before applying it.
- Clean speech for clarity without making it brittle or over-compressed.
- Use credible, energetic instrumental music. Raise it only until it supports momentum; it must remain below speech and follow voice ducking where supported.
- Use light flip/whoosh sounds on card entries and subtle pops/dings on meaningful reveals. Do not add SFX to every movement.
- Avoid crossfades between stock clips when they expose a flash of underlying A-roll or create a strange intermediate frame. Prefer clean cuts or a purpose-built transition that has been visually verified.

## Identity, disclaimer, and CTA

- Approved name tag: `陳譽` / `Dr Delex Chan`.
- Unless identity is already unmistakable in the frame, add Dr Chan attribution in the opening package or first clean identity beat; do not leave finished reels unsigned.
- Show the name tag near the opening when identity is not already obvious.
- Default medical disclaimer for the first and last three seconds when the current brief calls for the established treatment:

  `⚠ 醫療資訊僅供參考。`

  `如有不適，請諮詢專業醫生。請勿自行停藥或更改療程。`

- Keep the disclaimer at the very bottom, readable but visually secondary, inside the platform safe area.
- Default closing CTA: `覺得有用？儲存＋分享` and `追蹤專頁｜唔好錯過下次 Live`.
